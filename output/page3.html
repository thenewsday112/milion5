Mukluk Fire evacuation area shrinks

📅 Aug 25, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →
An Alaska Division of Forestry & Fire Protection Gannet Glacier crew member carries an armload of spruce branches on Aug. 24, 2026 while clearing an area where the Mukluk Fire burned in Tok. Photo by J. Hale/CIMT
  • Acres: 7,260 
  • Containment: 29% 
  • Location: Tok, AK 
  • Start Date: Aug. 17, 2026 
  • Fuels: Black Spruce, Shrubs, Grass 
  • Personnel:  350 

Highlights: Evacuation levels have changed, reducing the Level 3 GO! to the properties within the Mukluk Fire footprint. Work progresses to ensure the area within the fire’s perimeter is safe to repopulate these neighborhoods once work is complete in the Midnight Sun Zone evacuation zone.  

Operations: The crews’ work is concentrated within the southern perimeter. Firefighters are gridding the area to systematically locate and extinguish remaining heat within 50 feet of structures, roads, and driveways. To do this, they are walking the ground to find smoldering vegetation, roots, or stumps and cooling those spots until they’re cold to the touch. They’re also identifying and cutting hazard trees, bucking usable wood and piling it, and gathering unsalvageable woody debris into piles for safe burning. Excavators are being used to remove larger debris from the burned area. 

Dozers built a containment line Monday directly along the southern edge of a finger on the eastern portion of the fire. The Nulato Hills Crew is laying hose along the line, working to extinguish hot spots on the fire’s edge. 

Weather & Fire Behavior: Tuesday will bring another shift in weather as a storm moves in from the Bering Sea. Expect breezy conditions with south winds gusting up to 25 mph in the afternoon and evening. Clouds will thicken by late evening, followed by light rain. That rain is forecast to continue into Wednesday morning.

Rain aids firefighters; safety threats remain on properties impacted by Mukluk Fire

📅 Aug 24, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →
The Tanana Chiefs Fire Crew Fire Crew clears out fire-damaged trees along the fire’s southern edge on Aug. 22, 2026. Photo by J. Hale/AKCIMT
  • Acres: 7,260
  • Containment: 29%
  • Location: Tok, AK
  • Start Date: Aug. 17, 2026
  • Fuels: Black Spruce, Shrubs, Grass
  • Personnel:  328

Highlights: Recent significant rainfall in the Mukluk Fire area allowed officials to lift all Level 1 READY and Level 2 SET evacuation zones on Sunday afternoon. Level 3 GO! Midnight Sun Zone on the north side of the Alaska Highway from Sun Dog Trail to 5th Street East and north of Red Fox Drive, remains in place. While the overall fire threat in this zone has decreased, hazard trees continue to pose serious safety risks for firefighters, emergency responders, and the public. 

Around 100 people attended a community meeting at Tok School Sunday evening, where fire, emergency response, and assistance officials provided updates on the current fire situation, the outlook, and available recovery support for those affected by the Mukluk Fire. A recording of the meeting is available on the Alaska Division of Forestry & Fire Protection YouTube channel and Facebook page. 

Alaska State Emergency Operations Center Public Information Officer Jeremy Zidek talks about what kind of assistance is available for victims of the Mukluk Fire at a community meeting at the Tok School on Aug. 23, 2026. Photo by J. Hale/CIMT

Operations: Firefighters took advantage of approximately half an inch of rain to make progress along the southern perimeter, which remains under evacuation. Crews are cooling hot spots, removing unstable trees, and clearing brush around homes and roadways. Work was briefly paused Sunday due to safety concerns brought on by wind and rain. Engine crews continue nighttime patrols in parts of Tok impacted by the fire to watch for any potential flare-ups. 

The Alaska Team is coordinating closely with Alaska Power & Telephone to bring utility workers into cleared areas as soon as conditions are safe. This careful, step‑by‑step process will improve safety and help restore power before residents are allowed to return to the evacuated area. 

Evacuations: Level 3 GO! (Red) for Midnight Sun Zone, the area on the north side of the Alaska Highway from Sun Dog Trail on the west to 5th Street East on the east to north of Red Fox Drive.  

Weather & Fire Behavior: After Sunday’s substantial rainfall, warmer and drier conditions return Monday before colder, wetter weather moves in Tuesday. The added moisture to the ground and heavy vegetation will limit fire spread, with activity expected to be mostly creeping, smoldering, and occasional isolated torching within the established perimeter. The fall‑like weather in the coming days will support firefighters as they work toward containment along the perimeter and around affected structures.

READY and SET evacuation levels removed on Mukluk Fire

📅 Aug 23, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

Evacuation levels are being lowered for the Mukluk Fire (#487) on Sunday afternoon. All of the Level 1 READY and Level 2 SET zones have been removed. The Level 3 GO! Midnight Sun Zone for the area on the north side of the Alaska Highway from Sun Dog Trail to 5th Street East, to north of Red Fox Drive, will remain. The threat of the fire is not as great, but hazard trees remain a real concern. Trees are hazardous because their root systems have been burned, allowing them to fall at any time. Fire crews are mopping up and clearing hazardous trees in a 50-foot area around homes that remain in the evacuated area. Firefighters are also clearing trees that can fall, blocking driveways and roads. Progress will be reported daily in morning updates, but the area will remain evacuated until the entire 866-acre area is complete. The public needs to keep in mind that hazards will remain outside the 50-foot area around homes. Fire-weakened trees will still fall unexpectedly. Ash pits, places where stumps and roots have burned completely, leaving a hole full of possibly hot ash that is not immediately obvious, will still be a hazard. Toxic and hazardous materials where homes have been destroyed will remain until the site is cleared after fire operations are complete.

A community meeting will be held at 6 p.m. on Sunday, Aug. 23, at the Tok School gymnasium.

MTLNF Lolo National Forest Prescribed Fire Operations

Last updated: 2026-11-01 --- The type of incident is Prescribed Fire and involves the following unit(s) Lolo National Forest. --- State: Montana --- Coordinates: Latitude: 46° 49 76 Longitude: 113° 55 73 --- NOTE: All fire perimeters and points are approximations. --- Incident Overview: 2026 Prescribed Burn Operations The Lolo National Forest, along with their interagency partners and neighbors, plans to implement prescribed fire projects, as weather and fuels conditions allow. Public and firefighter safety is the top priority in all prescribed fire operations. Fire managers develop burn plans accounting for safety, specific fuel and weather prescriptions, and smoke management. Prior to implementation, local fire managers coordinate with local cooperators, counties, and partners.  All prescribed burns are closely evaluated and are only approved when favorable conditions are present.Benefits of prescribed fire as a forest management tool:Protects communities and infrastructure by reducing hazardous fuels and the risk of future high-intensity wildfires.Mitigates future wildfire risk and promote resilient fire-adapted landscapes.Reduces build-up of flammable vegetation, dead and down trees, and overgrowth.Improves and supports wildlife habitat for many species on the Forest.Limits the spread of invasive plant species and minimizes the spread of pest insects and disease, maintaining native ecosystems.Recycles nutrients back into the soil.Promotes the growth of trees, plants, wildflowers, and other flora and fauna.Continues the historic, natural fire regime of periodic disturbance by fire in forested ecosystems. Prescribed fires are planned for various units across the Forest; however, the window of opportunity for prescribed fire implementation is affected by several factors. Safety factors, weather conditions, air quality, personnel availability, and environmental regulations are continually monitored before implementation to determine feasibility of moving forward with the prescribed fire operations, during implementation, and after completion of the unit(s). Types of Prescribed FirePile Burning: ignition of hand or machine piles of cut vegetation that have been allowed to dry out. Pile burning is commonly used to mitigate wildfire risk in areas where broadcast burning may not be feasible, such as adjacent to communities or private lands. Piles are made from the residual woody debris often left after a mechanical thinning treatment and can range in size from small hand stacked piles to large piles created with mechanical equipment. Piles are typically burned in fall, winter, or early spring after the piles have dried out and conditions allow, reducing the damage to the remaining trees and confining the majority of fire to the footprint of the pile.Under-burning: a type of prescribed fire treatment, that ignites vegetation under the forest canopy and focuses on the consumption of surface fuels.
