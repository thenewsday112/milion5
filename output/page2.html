These conditions are monitored closely before and during burning. Mukluk Fire reaches 70% containment as firefighters work hotspots near Midnight Sun Drive

📅 Sep 12, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →
  • Acres: 7,339
  • Containment: 70%
  • Location: Tok, Alaska
  • Start Date: Aug. 17, 2026
  • Fuels: Black Spruce, Shrubs, Grass
  • Personnel: 31
Members of the TCC crew gather for a crew photo during suppression repair on the Mukluk Fire. Photo/ Katherine Bollen

Firefighters extinguished a long-smoldering hotspot near a home on Midnight Sun Drive Friday morning. An engine and members of the Tanana Chiefs Conference Type 2 Initial Attack Crew responded after smoke was observed coming from a berm of soil, logs and vegetation.

Heat remained in a large log buried inside the berm and continued to smolder beneath the surface. After several weeks, smoke became visible at multiple locations along the berm. Firefighters removed and cut apart the logs, applied water and cleared an area approximately 10 feet wide, 30 feet long and 4 feet deep to extinguish the remaining heat.

Members of the TCC crew work along the berm, digging through soil and woody debris to locate additional heat and smoldering material. Photo DFFP

Pockets of heat may continue to appear as fall progresses. Rain and cooler temperatures can reduce surface fire activity but may not reach heat buried deep within logs, roots or berms. If you see fire or smoke, call 911 so firefighters can investigate and extinguish the heat before it has an opportunity to spread. Do not call the local forestry office to report a fire.

The TCC crew continued suppression repair Friday. Working alongside Resource Advisors, and excavators, firefighters moved along dozer lines and pulled displaced soil, brush and vegetation back across areas cleared by dozers during active suppression. This work helps rehabilitate areas that were disturbed while firefighters were constructing containment lines.

An excavator pulls dirt and vegetation over a turnout near a containment line constructed by a dozer during active suppression of the Mukluk Fire. Photo by Drew Anderson

Recent wind and rain have further weakened burned and charred trees throughout the fire area. Many have little to no root support remaining and may fall without warning. One falling tree can also knock down surrounding trees.

Fire managers continue to urge hunters and residents to remain outside the fire perimeter, particularly north of Red Fox Drive and near the uncontained northern edge. Falling trees, ash pits, unstable ground and lingering hotspots remain hazards. Staying out of the area also gives firefighters and equipment room to safely complete work along roads and dozer lines north of Red Fox Drive.

Map of Mukluk Fire perimeter (#487) Located in Tok. Click Image for PDF

Weather & Fire Behavior: Cooler weather and rain are forecast Saturday, with temperatures reaching the mid-50s. Winds around 10 mph are expected to become light during the day. Sunday should be mostly dry, partly cloudy and high temperatures in the mid-50s. Overnight temperatures may fall to around 30 degrees. Rain will help cool exposed fuels, but heat buried deep within logs, roots and berms may remain.

Fire season remains in effect in the Delta, Tok and Copper River protection areas through 11:59 p.m. Monday, September 14. Burn permits are still required, and anyone planning to burn must confirm that burning is allowed before beginning. Check permit status at https://forestry.alaska.gov/burn/.

Email: 2026.Mukluk@firenet.gov

Falling trees shift Mukluk Fire operations while flareups remain within perimeter

📅 Sep 10, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

Acres: 7,339
Containment: 45%
Location: Tok, Alaska
Start Date: Aug. 17, 2026
Fuels: Black Spruce, Shrubs, Grass
Personnel: 31

Strong winds Wednesday forced firefighters to pause planned suppression repair along the western flank north of Red Fox Drive. Trees within the burned area have little or no root system remaining in some locations and were falling with little warning. Crews moved out of the timber and shifted their work to removing trees that had fallen across roadways and watching for trees threatening power lines. More than two dozen trees were reported down on power lines across Tok, including green trees outside the burned area. Alaska Power & Telephone shut off power to affected lines so firefighters and utility crews could respond safely.

Strong winds downed numerous trees, including trees that fell across roads and power lines. Photo J. Moser

Residents should remain alert for fallen trees and power lines throughout Tok. Stay away from any downed line or tree touching a power line and do not attempt to remove it.

Gusty winds also caused some remaining hotspots along the fire’s western flank to flare up, making smoke visible from town. The activity remained within previously burned portions of the existing perimeter, and the Mukluk Fire has not grown since Aug. 27.

Firefighters work to extinguish a flareup near Red Fox Drive on Sept. 9. Photo J. Moser

If you see fire or smoke, call 911 so it can be reported and dispatched through the proper emergency channels. Do not call the local forestry office to report a fire.

On Thursday, the Tanana Chiefs Conference Type 2 Initial Attack Crew and two excavators will assess the western edge of the fire and resume suppression repair where conditions are safe. Firefighters will continue patrolling for hotspots and removing hazardous trees when it is safe to do so.

The burned area north of Red Fox Drive remains unsafe. With hunting season underway, fire managers continue to urge hunters and others traveling near the western edge of the fire to stay out of this area. Hazards include falling trees, ash pits, unstable ground and pockets of heat.

Residents cleaning fire damaged property should not bury hazardous materials or contaminated debris. Contaminants can move through the ground and affect nearby wells, including wells serving neighboring properties. The Alaska Division of Homeland Security and Emergency Management and Team Rubicon continue to assist affected landowners. Recovery applications, debris disposal guidance and additional cleanup information are available at https://linktr.ee/2026MuklukFire.

Map of the Mukluk Fire in Tok. Click image for PDF

Weather & Fire Behavior: Winds are forecast to be much lighter Thursday, with northwest winds around 5 mph. Isolated early morning showers will give way to mostly cloudy skies, with a high near 54 degrees. Temperatures are expected to warm Friday, reaching near 68 degrees with light winds. Some rain fell over the fire Wednesday, but pockets of heat may continue to smolder and produce smoke.

Fire season remains in effect in the Delta, Tok and Copper River protection areas through 11:59 p.m. Monday, September 14. Burn permits are still required, and anyone planning to burn must confirm that burning is allowed before beginning. Check permit status at https://forestry.alaska.gov/burn/.

Updated structure assessment confirms 50 homes destroyed in Mukluk Fire

📅 Aug 30, 2026  |  Category: Travel  |  Read Source →
A Mat-Su engine task force made up of West Lakes Fire Department, Willow Caswell Fire Department, and Butte Fire Department firefighters burn piles of unsalvageable woody debris created during the removal of fire-weakened trees and brush. Photo taken on Aug. 29, 2026 by J. Hale/AKCIMT
  • Acres: 7,335 
  • Containment: 29% 
  • Location: Tok, AK 
  • Start Date: Aug. 17, 2026 
  • Fuels: Black Spruce, Shrubs, Grass 
  • Personnel:  261 

A small task force of firefighters spent the past several days conducting an assessment of structure loss. It has been determined that 50 single residences have been destroyed, as well as 108 outbuildings, garages, sheds, workshops, or other miscellaneous secondary structures.  

Personnel assigned to the Mukluk Fire continue to decrease as firefighters work to retrieve equipment from the field. Some of the gear must be accessed by boat or helicopter in remote sections of the fire area. This includes gathering hoses, pumps, and other equipment; cleaning, sorting, and packaging it for transport back to fire warehouses. Once returned, it will be cleaned, repaired, and refurbished so it’s ready for future assignments. Crews, engines, and heavy equipment remain on the incident, improving containment lines in Tok and burning woody debris piles created during the removal of fire‑weakened trees and brush. 

It’s important for anyone in or near the fire area to stay alert to potential hazards and steer clear of working firefighters. 

The Alaska Division of Forestry & Fire Protection’s Gannet Glacier Fire Crew is being released from the incident and will travel to the Lower 48. The Nulato Hills Type 2 Contract Crew, with firefighters from Selawik and Nulato, is also demobilizing on Sunday. 

People in the fire-impacted area are urged to avoid any hazardous materials that may remain in the debris. Burned structures, vehicles, and other debris can leave behind ash, sharp metal, chemicals, and other dangerous substances that should not be handled without proper protective equipment. Protect yourself by wearing sturdy footwear, gloves, long sleeves and long pants, eye protection, and respiratory protection such as an N-95 mask as appropriate when working around ash, debris, and damaged materials. 

The Alaska Division of Homeland Security and Emergency Management is working with the Alaska Department of Environmental Conservation Solid Waste Program to develop a plan to support property owners as they clean up fire-damaged and destroyed items. 

The Alaska Division of Forestry & Fire Protection (DFFP) is extending fire season in the Delta, Tok and Copper River protection areas due to continued dry conditions and an increased risk of wildland fire. The extension begins Tuesday, Sept. 1, and continues through 11:59 p.m. Monday, Sept. 14.  

Weather & Fire Behavior:  The fire area received only 0.05 inch of rain on Saturday and saw northwest wind gusts up to 30 mph. Conditions will improve on Sunday with sunny, warm weather and light winds, giving firefighters a favorable window for progress before colder, blustery weather arrives on Monday. Higher humidity, shorter daylight hours, and the shift toward fall-like conditions should help keep the fire within its current footprint.

Highlights: Around 250 firefighters, supported by numerous engines and heavy equipment, are working throughout the neighborhoods impacted by the Mukluk Fire. This surge of personnel and machinery reflects strong momentum and a focused plan to make the area safe for residents before they return. Crews are conducting aggressive mop‑up operations, aided by the .15 inches of rain the area has received since Tuesday afternoon and cooler, wetter weather in the forecast. With so many resources in the neighborhood, residents are asked to steer clear so firefighters can work safely and efficiently as they prepare the area for eventual reentry and damage assessment. 

Operations: Firefighters from Yukon, Canada demobilized Tuesday after providing vital support during the early and intense days of the Mukluk Fire. The Alaska Team has established a dedicated utilities group that is coordinating directly with Alaska Power & Telephone to support safe and efficient infrastructure repairs.

 

An excavator clears fire-damaged trees and brush from a utility corridor on Aug. 24, 2026. Photo by J. Hale/CIMT

A small contingent of engines continues to patrol the eastern and western edges, while Uncrewed Aerial Systems equipped with infrared cameras search for any remaining heat in these areas. 

Weather & Fire Behavior: Tuesday’s breezy conditions brought down a few trees before winds settled when rain arrived in the afternoon. As the cold front moves out on Wednesday, another period of gusty west to northwest winds is expected. With shorter daylight hours and cooler temperatures setting in, fire activity should remain low, limited to smoldering and creeping with the possibility of an occasional isolated torching tree.
