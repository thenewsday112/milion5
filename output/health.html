CAKNF Klamath National Forest Fall Prescribed Burning

📅 Oct 21, 2026  |  Category: Health  |  Read Source →
Last updated: 2026-10-29 --- The type of incident is Prescribed Fire and involves the following unit(s) Klamath National Forest. --- State: California --- Coordinates: Latitude: 41° 42 24 Longitude: 122° 38 40 --- NOTE: All fire perimeters and points are approximations. --- Incident Overview: Klamath National Forest prepares for fall prescribed burningRelease Date: October 1, 2026 (YREKA, Calif., October 1, 2026) — Cooler temperatures and changing fall conditions are creating opportunities for the Klamath National Forest to conduct prescribed underburning as weather and fuel conditions allow.Prescribed fire is an important tool used by the Klamath National Forest to reduce hazardous fuels, protect communities, improve wildlife habitat and forest health, reduce the potential severity of future wildfires, and promote public and firefighter safety.Prescribed underburning is the intentional application of low- to moderate-intensity fire under carefully selected weather and fuel conditions. Before a prescribed burn is conducted, fire managers develop a detailed burn plan that identifies the specific conditions, or prescription, needed to safely meet project objectives.“Prescribed fire requires careful planning and close coordination with weather and fuel conditions,” said Klamath National Forest Supervisor Chris L. Christofferson. “Our fire managers are prepared to take advantage of appropriate burn windows when conditions are favorable and it is safe to do so.”Burn prescriptions consider factors such as temperature, humidity, wind speed and direction, fuel moisture and expected fire behavior. These conditions are monitored closely before and during burning. Fire managers also consider wind direction and other factors to minimize smoke impacts to nearby communities. The Klamath National Forest has several prescribed burn units prepared across the forest and will conduct burning when conditions meet established prescriptions.Goosenest Ranger DistrictCedar Mountain: 600 acres, approximately 10 miles southeast of Macdoel.First Creek: 224 acres, just north of Grass Lake.Military Pass Road: 1.5 acres to improve habitat for the rare Cooke’s phacelia plant.Salmon-Scott River Ranger DistrictHayden Ridge: 240 acres, approximately 3 miles north of Callahan.Jess: 300 acres, approximately one-half mile south of Sawyers Bar.Scott Bar Mountain: 160 acres, approximately 12 miles west of Fort Jones.Happy Camp Oak Knoll Ranger DistrictCade Mountain: 110 acres, just east of Happy Camp.Prescribed burning may result in visible smoke in and around project areas. Smoke may linger for a day or two following ignition, depending on weather conditions. Residents and visitors can monitor local air quality at AirNow’s Fire and Smoke Map - https://fire.airnow.gov/#7.79/41.526/-122.645.Following a widespread wetting rain or snow event that effectively ends the fire season, fire managers will transition to pile burning. Pile-burning units are located throughout the forest and encompass thousands of acres.Fire managers will continue to monitor weather and fuel conditions to identify suitable opportunities for prescribed burning. Project information, including updates on planned burning, will be shared through the Klamath National Forest Facebook - https://www.facebook.com/KlamathNF and updated on this page.The public is encouraged to stay informed and use caution when traveling near prescribed fire operations.

COARF Salt Cabin Prescribed Burn

📅 Jan 12, 2026  |  Category: Health  |  Read Source →
Last updated: 2026-11-01 --- The type of incident is Prescribed Fire and involves the following unit(s) Arapaho and Roosevelt National Forests and Pawnee National Grassland. --- State: Colorado --- Coordinates: Latitude: 40° 40 28.56 Longitude: 105 ° 31 11.97 --- NOTE: All fire perimeters and points are approximations. --- Incident Overview: Roosevelt National Forest’s Canyon Lakes Ranger District is planning prescribed fire operations as soon as mid-October at the Salt Cabin Project area along CO State Hwy 14, in the Poudre Canyon. This work could continue throughout the fall as conditions allow. The goal of this prescribed fire is to reduce exposure to people, communities, infrastructure, and natural resources from the risk of catastrophic wildfire. Prescribed fire is used help reduce overgrown vegetation, to maintain and restore the condition and function of ecosystems and to reduce wildfire risk. This work, in turn, assists firefighters in protecting communities, property, and infrastructure in the event of wildfire.The project is located south of Rustic and CO State Hwy 14 and is a part of the overall strategy to confront the wildfire risk in Colorado's Front Range landscape.  This project will improve timber stands through thinning and prescribed burning to reduce natural fuel build-up and hazards, which is consistent with Forest-wide management goals and objectives. The Forest Service is proposing to re-introduce fire to forests that are adapted to low- and mixed-severity fires and maintain low fire hazard conditions created by both a previous prescribed fire (Dadd-Bennett) and the Cameron Peak Fire of 2020. The total project area encompasses 4,567 acres. Weather conditions are evaluated in the days and hours prior to ignitions. If conditions warrant, scheduled prescribed fire activities may be canceled. Watch for informational signage along roads near all prescribed fire areas before and during burns. County emergency management officials will be notified when burning begins.  Residents and visitors to the Pingree Park/CR 63E and Cache La Poudre River corridors may experience smoke during prescribed burns. Prescribed fire smoke may affect your health. For more information, visit wood smoke and your health. When driving, slow down and turn on your headlights when you encounter smoke on the road.For up-to-date information on prescribed fire activity, follow us on Facebook and X, visit our website, or check this Inciweb page.

We estimate this work to continue through the winter as weather conditions allow. The forests in Southwest Colorado are part of a fire-adapted ecosystem, which historically experienced frequent, low intensity fires on a large scale. Prescribed fire replicates that fire regime and increases the area on our landscape that has been burned at low and moderate conditions. These efforts reduce hazardous ground fuels, lessen the risk of unplanned large-scale wildfire, help restore ponderosa pine ecosystems, and improve wildlife habitat. Prescribed fire also reduces the ladder fuels that can carry fire into the canopy, killing mature trees. Prescribed fire smoke may affect your health.
