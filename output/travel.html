Updated structure assessment confirms 50 homes destroyed in Mukluk Fire

📅 Aug 30, 2026  |  Category: Travel  |  Read Source →
A Mat-Su engine task force made up of West Lakes Fire Department, Willow Caswell Fire Department, and Butte Fire Department firefighters burn piles of unsalvageable woody debris created during the removal of fire-weakened trees and brush. Photo taken on Aug. 29, 2026 by J. Hale/AKCIMT
  • Acres: 7,335 
  • Containment: 29% 
  • Location: Tok, AK 
  • Start Date: Aug. 17, 2026 
  • Fuels: Black Spruce, Shrubs, Grass 
  • Personnel:  261 

A small task force of firefighters spent the past several days conducting an assessment of structure loss. It has been determined that 50 single residences have been destroyed, as well as 108 outbuildings, garages, sheds, workshops, or other miscellaneous secondary structures.  

Personnel assigned to the Mukluk Fire continue to decrease as firefighters work to retrieve equipment from the field. Some of the gear must be accessed by boat or helicopter in remote sections of the fire area. This includes gathering hoses, pumps, and other equipment; cleaning, sorting, and packaging it for transport back to fire warehouses. Once returned, it will be cleaned, repaired, and refurbished so it’s ready for future assignments. Crews, engines, and heavy equipment remain on the incident, improving containment lines in Tok and burning woody debris piles created during the removal of fire‑weakened trees and brush. 

It’s important for anyone in or near the fire area to stay alert to potential hazards and steer clear of working firefighters. 

The Alaska Division of Forestry & Fire Protection’s Gannet Glacier Fire Crew is being released from the incident and will travel to the Lower 48. The Nulato Hills Type 2 Contract Crew, with firefighters from Selawik and Nulato, is also demobilizing on Sunday. 

People in the fire-impacted area are urged to avoid any hazardous materials that may remain in the debris. Burned structures, vehicles, and other debris can leave behind ash, sharp metal, chemicals, and other dangerous substances that should not be handled without proper protective equipment. Protect yourself by wearing sturdy footwear, gloves, long sleeves and long pants, eye protection, and respiratory protection such as an N-95 mask as appropriate when working around ash, debris, and damaged materials. 

The Alaska Division of Homeland Security and Emergency Management is working with the Alaska Department of Environmental Conservation Solid Waste Program to develop a plan to support property owners as they clean up fire-damaged and destroyed items. 

The Alaska Division of Forestry & Fire Protection (DFFP) is extending fire season in the Delta, Tok and Copper River protection areas due to continued dry conditions and an increased risk of wildland fire. The extension begins Tuesday, Sept. 1, and continues through 11:59 p.m. Monday, Sept. 14.  

Weather & Fire Behavior:  The fire area received only 0.05 inch of rain on Saturday and saw northwest wind gusts up to 30 mph. Conditions will improve on Sunday with sunny, warm weather and light winds, giving firefighters a favorable window for progress before colder, blustery weather arrives on Monday. Higher humidity, shorter daylight hours, and the shift toward fall-like conditions should help keep the fire within its current footprint.

Mukluk Fire evacuations to lift on Saturday

📅 Aug 28, 2026  |  Category: Travel  |  Read Source →
  • Acres: 7,335 
  • Containment: 29% 
  • Location: Tok, AK 
  • Start Date: Aug. 17, 2026 
  • Fuels: Black Spruce, Shrubs, Grass 
  • Personnel:  323 
A Gannet Glacier crewmember cuts hazard trees along a road in the community impacted by the Mukluk Fire on Aug. 22, 2026. Photo by J. Hale/AKCIMT

Highlights:  Firefighters are finishing work to stabilize natural hazards in burned neighborhoods along the southern edge of the Mukluk Fire on Friday. Officials plan to lift the remaining evacuation orders on Saturday and let residents return home. With lingering hot spots extinguished and unstable trees removed within 50 feet of structures, driveways, and roads, crews can now shift their focus away from active fire suppression. The next phase will focus on retrieving equipment, disposing of woody debris, and repairing areas disturbed during efforts to protect Tok and the surrounding area. Please give firefighters plenty of space so they can work safely. 

People need to watch for unstable trees, weakened root systems, ash pits and unstable or eroded ground – especially after wind or rain. Protect yourself by wearing sturdy footwear, gloves, eye protection and respiratory protection as appropriate when working around ash, debris and damaged materials.  

Residents returning to fire-impacted properties are urged to avoid any hazardous materials that may remain in the debris. Burned structures, vehicles, and other debris can leave behind ash, sharp metal, chemicals, and other dangerous substances that should not be handled without proper protective equipment. The Alaska Division of Homeland Security and Emergency Management is preparing a plan to support property owners with debris removal. 

The Alaska Division of Forestry & Fire Protection (DFFP) is extending fire season in the Delta, Tok and Copper River protection areas due to continued dry conditions and an increased risk of wildland fire. The extension begins Tuesday, Sept. 1, and continues through 11:59 p.m. Monday, Sept. 14. There is also burn permit suspension in place for Tok Protection Area.  

Operations:  The U.S. Wildland Fire Service Midnight Sun Hotshots from Fairbanks, along with the U.S. Forest Service Smith River and Redding Hotshots from California, have completed their assignments and will travel to the Lower 48, where they are urgently needed during an increasingly volatile fire season. Numerous crews, engines, and heavy equipment will remain to continue suppression repair work. 

Weather & Fire Behavior:  Friday’s forecast calls for breezy, overcast conditions with scattered showers that are not expected to produce any meaningful wetting rain. Fire behavior will remain subdued, limited to smoldering and creeping, and will stay contained within the fire’s current footprint.
